# Resource Scarcity

## Overview

You have 3 bullets. There are 5 enemies. Do you fight, sneak, or run? Resource scarcity is the mechanic that makes survival games tense. When resources are limited, every decision matters. Every bullet fired is a choice. Every health pack used is a commitment. It transforms "I can fight everything" into "can I afford to fight THIS?"

## Core loop

1. Player starts with limited resources
2. Resources are consumed through gameplay (ammo, health, fuel)
3. Finding resources is rare and rewarding
4. Player must choose how to allocate scarce resources
5. Tension builds as resources deplete

## Scarcity types

### Ammo scarcity (Resident Evil)
- **Limited bullets**: Every shot counts
- **Enemy design**: Some enemies aren't worth shooting
- **Avoidance encouraged**: Stealth and running are valid
- **Crafting**: Combine herbs, find gunpowder

### Food/water (Subnautica, The Long Dark)
- **Constant drain**: Hunger/thirst depletes over time
- **Risk/reward**: Better food is in dangerous areas
- **Planning ahead**: Need to gather before exploring
- **Environmental pressure**: Cold, depth, darkness

### Material scarcity (This War of Mine)
- **Trading**: Resources for crafting, food for survival
- **Moral choices**: Steal from others or scavenge dangerous areas
- **Character needs**: Multiple characters need feeding
- **Winter pressure**: Resources become scarcer over time

### Fuel/power (Subnautica)
- **Oxygen**: Limited exploration time underwater
- **Power**: Bases need energy to function
- **Vehicle fuel**: Can't go far without refueling
- **Progression gate**: Better tech = less scarcity

## Design dimensions

### Scarcity level
- **Extreme** (This War of Mine): Every item is precious
- **High** (Resident Evil): Ammo is limited but manageable
- **Moderate** (Subnautica): Resources are available but require effort
- **Low** (some survival games): Resources are abundant

### Finding resources
- **Scavenging**: Search the environment thoroughly
- **Crafting**: Convert raw materials into useful items
- **Trading**: Exchange with NPCs or other players
- **Farming**: Renewable resource generation
- **Risk-based**: Better resources in dangerous locations

### Decision points
- **Fight or flight**: Use ammo or avoid combat
- **Explore or conserve**: Go further or save resources
- **Help or survive**: Share resources or hoard
- **Craft or save**: Use materials now or save for later

### Tension generation
- **Visible inventory**: Players see their dwindling supplies
- **Audio cues**: Low ammo sounds, health warnings
- **Environmental pressure**: Enemies, weather, time
- **Consequences**: Running out means death or failure

## Anti-patterns

- **False scarcity**: Resources are actually abundant, just hidden
- **Too punishing**: Running out of resources = permanent death = frustration
- **No tension**: If resources are always sufficient, scarcity is meaningless
- **Tedious gathering**: If finding resources is boring, the mechanic fails
- **Illusion of choice**: If one resource type is always the answer

## References

- Resident Evil: The gold standard for ammo scarcity in survival horror
- Subnautica: Oxygen and power scarcity create underwater tension
- This War of Mine: Moral scarcity — choosing who eats and who doesn't
- The Long Dark: Environmental scarcity — cold, hunger, and isolation
