# Environmental Storytelling

## Overview

The most powerful stories in games are often the ones never explicitly told. A room that tells you everything about who lived there. A battlefield that shows you what happened without a single word of dialogue. Environmental storytelling turns players into detectives and every scene into a mystery.

## Core loop

1. Player enters a space
2. Player notices environmental details
3. Player infers what happened from clues
4. Player builds mental narrative from evidence
5. Discovery feels personal and rewarding

## Storytelling techniques

### Object placement
- **Character artifacts**: Personal items tell who was here
- **Activity evidence**: Signs of what people were doing
- **Temporal clues**: Items show when things happened
- **Relationship indicators**: Objects together tell stories

### Architecture & layout
- **Room function**: What was this space used for?
- **Damage patterns**: What destroyed this place?
- **Modifications**: How did inhabitants change the space?
- **Abandonment clues**: Why did they leave?

### Atmospheric cues
- **Lighting**: Mood and time of day
- **Sound design**: Environmental audio tells stories
- **Weather**: Elements show passage of time
- **Growth/decay**: Nature reclaiming spaces

### Implicit narrative
- **Before/after**: Show change without explaining it
- **Cause/effect**: Evidence of consequences
- **Mystery**: Leave gaps for player imagination
- **Multiple interpretations**: Ambiguity creates discussion

## Design dimensions

### Detail density
- **Dense** (What Remains of Edith Finch): Every corner has story
- **Moderate** (Bioshock): Key areas tell stories, spaces between are breathing room
- **Sparse** (Dark Souls): Rare but impactful discoveries
- **Ambient** (The Last of Us): Story is woven into the environment

### Player agency
- **Active discovery**: Player must look for clues
- **Passive observation**: Story is in plain sight
- **Interactive**: Player can examine/interact with story objects
- **Forced**: Camera guides player to story elements

### Narrative complexity
- **Single story**: One clear narrative from environment
- **Multiple layers**: Different details tell different stories
- **Unreliable**: Environment may be misleading
- **Collaborative**: Players piece together different interpretations

## Anti-patterns

- **Too obvious**: If story is spoon-fed, discovery is lost
- **Too obscure**: If clues are impossible to find, story is lost
- **Inconsistent**: Environmental story contradicts explicit narrative
- **Decorative**: Pretty environments with no story purpose
- **Text-heavy**: Notes and logs everywhere = reading, not discovering

## References

- Dark Souls: Item descriptions + environment = deep lore
